MDWPhys: Lightweight Temporal-Frequency Dynamic Modeling for Noise-Robust rPPG Estimation

Chentao Xu, Zhaoyang Yang*

IEEE Sensors Journal

Abstract

Remote photoplethysmography (rPPG) has broad potential for non-contact health monitoring. However, rPPG estimation in realistic scenarios is strongly affected by multiple sources of non-stationary noise. Head motion and facial expressions cause local spatial misalignment, while illumination variations introduce dynamic chromatic disturbances. In addition, the quadratic complexity of conventional self-attention limits efficient deployment for long sequences. To address these challenges, we propose MDWPhys, a lightweight spatial-temporal-frequency dynamic modeling approach for rPPG waveform estimation. The proposed Motion-Aware Feature Encoder (MAFE) processes both raw facial frames and neighborhood differences between adjacent frames to characterize and suppress local spatial non-stationary noise caused by head motion and facial expressions. Spatial Attention Aggregation (SAA) then learns spatial weights according to the signal and noise characteristics of different facial regions, suppresses noise-contaminated areas, and aggregates informative spatial features into a feature sequence. For temporal modeling, the Multi-Temporal DeltaNet (MT-DeltaNet) uses linear attention and parameter-shared temporally shifted paths to aggregate information from different temporal contexts. The Wavelet Feed-Forward Network (Wavelet FFN) further separates low-frequency physiological trends from high-frequency non-stationary noise in the frequency domain and adaptively attenuates noisy components through learnable soft thresholding. Intra-dataset experiments on PURE, UBFC-rPPG, iBVP, and SCAMPS, together with cross-dataset evaluations under multiple training and testing settings, demonstrate that MDWPhys effectively improves robustness to multiple sources of non-stationary noise and achieves competitive estimation performance with low inference latency.

PhysLNN: A Temporally Enhanced Liquid Neural Network for Robust Heart Rate Estimation Based on Remote Photoplethysmography

Chentao Xu, Zhaoyang Yang*, Huamin Ge, Yanfei Shen

Physiological Measurement

Abstract

Objective. Remote photoplethysmography (rPPG) enables non-contact heart rate estimation from facial videos, but existing models often face a trade-off between robustness and computational efficiency. This study aims to develop a robust and resource-efficient framework for non-contact heart rate estimation applications.

Approach. PhysLNN uses a streamlined front end that fuses input-level normalized frame differences with feature-level temporal difference convolution in a compact 3D-CNN pipeline. This fusion embeds temporal enhancement into spatiotemporal feature extraction and supplies pulse-related features to an NCP-wired liquid module. The sparse NCP structure incorporates a compact recurrent bottleneck. Liquid Time-Constant (LTC) and Closed-form Continuous-time (CfC) units provide two variants.

Main results. Evaluation on three public datasets, PURE, UBFC-rPPG, and iBVP, shows competitive HR estimation accuracy, with cross-dataset performance varying across transfer directions. In intra-dataset testing on PURE, PhysLNN obtains a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 0.14 bpm. In cross-dataset testing, PhysLNN demonstrates strong generalization in selected transfer directions. For example, PhysLNN-LTC achieves MAEs of 0.30 bpm and 0.26 bpm when transferred to PURE from UBFC-rPPG and iBVP, respectively. Compared with mainstream methods, PhysLNN also requires fewer parameters, multiply-accumulate operations, and GPU memory.

Significance. The results indicate that continuous-time dynamic modeling can model rPPG signals while maintaining computational efficiency. PhysLNN provides a robust and lightweight solution for video-based physiological measurement, supporting accurate and resource-efficient heart rate monitoring from facial videos.
